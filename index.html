Station III · Repeat
Draw it a thousand times

Keep the strategy and size chosen above. Each draw gives one sample mean. The histogram collects them.

Central limit theorem

Each draw gives one sample mean. The histogram above shows where 1,000 of them landed. Three things hold for a probability sample, whatever the shape of the population.

The means centre on the parameter. Compare the mean of means in the boxes with the parameter once revealed.

The means are close to normally distributed, although the weights in the orchard are skewed to the right. Lay the normal curve over to check.

Their spread is the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size, σ/√n. The box for sd of means sits beside σ/√n so the two can be read together. Four times the sample halves the spread.

These three hold when each apple is drawn independently from the same population, written iid. Trees and neighbours break independence, and the bell changes shape.

Law of large numbers

A different picture: one simple random sample grows from 1 apple to 1,000, and the running mean settles on the parameter.
